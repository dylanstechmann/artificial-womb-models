# Holm et al. 2017 amino-acid exchange source screen — 2026-10-10

## Result

This is a useful public, aggregate in-vivo reference for amino-acid concentrations and paired arteriovenous differences at term, but its participant-level NSD file is access-restricted. Keep this as a distinct human physiology comparator, not as an ex-vivo perfusion dataset or an observation-level intake. No restricted file was downloaded or requested, and no author was contacted.

## Evidence and access findings

The paper describes a cross-sectional in-vivo study of 179 healthy women with uncomplicated term pregnancies. At planned cesarean delivery, the investigators sampled maternal radial artery and uterine vein, plus fetal umbilical vein and artery, and measured 19 amino acids by LC-MS/MS. The article provides group medians and quartiles for vessel concentrations and paired concentration differences, including a water-transfer adjustment. The supporting table summarizes correlations. The paper notes incomplete vessel sampling and uses available pairs, so the cohort size must not be treated as the sample size for every amino-acid comparison.

The article reports positive umbilical venoarterial concentration differences for 14 amino acids after adjustment for placental water transfer, and describes fetal-placental supply as involving placental and fetal metabolism and interconversion. These are concentration differences, not mass-transfer rates: the inspected public tables do not provide paired blood-flow measurements needed to turn them into fluxes. Maternal radial-artery-to-uterine-vein differences also include possible uterine contributions, which the authors acknowledge.

The structured Table 2 extract preserves the vessel-specific N row and the literal median [Q1, Q3] source-cell string. One source cell reads `Alanine, uterine vein: 255 [2, 294]`; the lower quartile appears unusual, so it is flagged and retained without correction. The separate Table 3 extract preserves 19 analytes across uteroplacental and umbilical circulations, crude and water-adjusted median differences, printed p-values, table-cell locators, and bold status. Table 3's N row is retained by circulation; do not treat it as analyte-specific denominators. Its footnote says bold differences mark significance after FDR correction, with adjusted p-values for significant findings reported in the article text; this intake does not recompute those results. S1 remains an aggregate correlation summary and was not transcribed into either CSV.

A descriptive consistency check of the extracted water-adjusted umbilical medians finds 17 positive and two negative values. Of the positive medians, 14 have source bolding for FDR significance; glutamic acid is the one negative bolded value. This matches the article's report of 14 significant positive amino-acid differences and a significant negative glutamic-acid difference. The three other positive medians (methionine, threonine, glycine) and serine's negative median are not bolded; do not treat sign alone as significance.

The paper's data-availability statement points to NSD V1. The repository's current V2 metadata labels the unit of analysis as an individual and explicitly limits data provision to agreement with the principal investigator upon order. This is controlled mediated access, not an open downloadable file. In keeping with the project instruction not to contact authors, the intake stops at the paper, supplement, and public repository metadata. Participant-level values, identifiers, missingness patterns, and reproducible paired calculations remain unassessed.

## Feasibility relevance and limits

This study is valuable as a term human in-vivo comparator for the distinction between maternal supply, uteroplacental exchange, and fetal-side net concentration differences. It cautions against treating maternal uptake as equivalent to fetal delivery and against treating concentration differences as flux or nutritional sufficiency. Its cross-sectional term sampling does not describe earlier developmental stages, artificial support, continuous gestation, birth outcomes under an artificial environment, or later health. Do not combine its aggregate values with ex-vivo perfusion trajectories or use them to calibrate a developmental-support model.
