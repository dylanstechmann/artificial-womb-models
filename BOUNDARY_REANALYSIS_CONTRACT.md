## Earlier coordination and later progression

The proposed target is whether earlier measured relative movement improves
prediction of a separately assessed later developmental endpoint. This is a
testable design hypothesis, not a result of the current source tables.

**Competing explanations:** coordination could contain additional information;
it could merely track existing growth; or both measurements could reflect
shared selection, imaging registration or segmentation error. A movement/length
association alone cannot distinguish these possibilities.

Required observations are source-established embryo/experiment identities,
original clocks and coordinate definitions, repeated boundary and embryo
measurements, and later individual outcomes with the starting/disposition
roster. The observation cutoff must precede the assessed endpoint. Unobserved,
excluded and failed units remain explicit, with reasons and timing when known.
Stage resemblance must remain separate from chronological time.

Compare an earlier-growth/stage baseline with the same baseline plus a
prespecified coordination measure. The added measure must use the earlier
observation window only. Absolute boundary and embryo motion share a reference;
registration error can make them move together. Qualification must establish
how relative movement and its uncertainty are recovered, including a common
reference error. No choice of lag, cutoff or feature may be selected on the
held-out endpoint results.

Split evaluation at the highest authenticated shared biological/experimental
group, keeping each embryo's cells, lines, planes and times together. If those
groups or enough independent units are unavailable, stop at descriptive
reproduction. Report baseline and added-measure errors, unavailable predictions
and outcome coverage; do not substitute a favorable aggregate curve.

Appendix S8 counts cells only among embryos classed as successfully developed:
14 downward from three replicates and six upward from four. Its reported upward
19% efficiency lacks a full starting denominator in that caption. Conditioning
on the endpoint changes the question and cannot establish prediction across all
starting embryos. Appendix S9 averages planes within three imaged embryos;
those planes cannot increase independent n. Neither branch supplies the missing
Figure 6I/J embryo/outcome map.

## Shape prediction and mechanically interpretable parameters

Appendix Note 2 averages parameters from two source embryos to compare with
the third, rotating the held-out embryo. The prediction still receives the
held-out measured volume derivative. Two contact-angle trajectories agree
within the reported three-SD bands; in the third, the measured angle falls
faster after source hour 44. Data bars use one SEM, and Table S4 parameter
errors use one SD. Preserve all three definitions and the mismatch.

This supports a conditional shape question: given the observed growth input,
how well does a mechanics account describe another embryo's angle dynamics?
It does not independently forecast growth or qualify an observer that uses
only past readings. A future forecast question needs an explicit past-only
growth input or separate growth prediction, with its uncertainty propagated.
Its comparison should include a simpler shape baseline using exactly the same
available inputs and cutoff. Neither comparison is run here.

The source uses a custom tension unit, gamma0, and notes incomplete information
about energy/mass quantities. Thus physical force scale remains unqualified.
As an elementary mathematical illustration, a ratio-only observable cannot
distinguish `(a, b)` from `(s*a, s*b)` for positive `s`: both have `a/b`.
This illustration uses no biological parameters and is not a proof of every
identifiability property of the source's dynamic model. It explains why a
dimensionless shape fit does not itself establish an absolute force or stiffness
specification. Identifying such a scale would require independently qualified
dimensional evidence and a reviewed measurement/model contract.

Model disagreement is an observation about predictive validity. It is not
itself a demonstrated adverse organism outcome. Even an improved shape fit
would leave support receipt, sustained exchange, later function and healthy
human development unresolved.

## Consequence for the machine concept

Machine A's remodelable interface remains a candidate because tissue growth and
boundary development must coexist. Machine B needs distinct observables for
earlier coordination, actual recipient support and later progression, with
source clocks, reference uncertainty and unit hierarchy. Shape alone cannot
serve as a calibrated force sensor or a health certificate.

The next eligible numerical question is the narrow endpoint relationship after
source review. The higher-value coordination question remains gated by the
individual trajectory/outcome map and complete disposition coverage. Changing
a model's complexity cannot recover those missing observations.
