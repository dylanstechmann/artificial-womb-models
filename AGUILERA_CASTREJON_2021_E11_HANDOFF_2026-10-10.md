# Aguilera-Castrejon et al. (2021): E11 handoff qualification

This source screen records the terminal handoff in the mouse whole-embryo ex-utero culture study, because the paper reaches late organogenesis but does not support a continuation claim. It is a source-level qualification, not a protocol or a proposed device specification.

The [Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03416-3) reports separate mouse cohorts beginning at E5.5, E6.5, and E7.5 and reaching approximately E11. For the E7.5-start cohort, the Results report approximately 77% normal development after four culture days across mouse strains. The same passage reports abnormalities, abrupt interruption of yolk-sac circulation, and pericardial effusion followed by rapid overnight death. The authors interpret the deterioration as consistent with oxygen and nutrient delivery no longer matching increased body size in the absence of maternal blood supply. That explanation is the authors' interpretation; the reported account does not isolate a single causal mechanism.

The captions matter for interpreting the rates. Figure 1d defines *n* as total embryos and *x* as experiments, with plotted values reported as mean ± s.e.m. Extended Data Figure 2b says parenthetical numbers are embryos assessed per condition at each sampled timepoint and that embryos dissected, fixed, or moved to another condition are subtracted from the total. Cycle 267 retrieved two publisher-hosted workbooks: MOESM11 Panel d reports E7.5 culture-efficiency totals of 227/230, 212/230, 155/185, and 126/163 at +Day 1 to +Day 4; the last is printed as 77%. MOESM12 Panel b reports E6.5 totals of 414/421 and 386/399 at +Day 1 and +Day 2. The experiment rows sum to each workbook aggregate. MOESM11 explicitly says dash rows were not continued due to experimental requirements; MOESM12 has a Day 2 dash and denominator change whose reasons are not stated. Two E6.5 experiment-row percentage literals disagree with their own counts. The denominators are therefore timepoint-specific assessed sets, not survival probabilities or a verified all-start cohort. The exact Nature page download-anchor-to-MOESM mapping remains unverified. The [candidate aggregate intake](config/observations/aguilera_castrejon_2021_source_data_candidate.json), source workbooks, per-cell crosswalk, and arithmetic receipt are pinned in the artifact folder; human review remains pending.

The linked [GEO series GSE149372](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE149372) has 20 sample records under BioProject PRJNA628518. Its embryo samples are labeled E8.5 or E10.5; the remaining records are stem-cell controls. The E8.5 “replicates 2 and 3” are each labeled as a three-embryo pool. No GEO sample is labeled E11 or later. This public molecular series therefore adds stage-specific snapshots but does not supply the E11 failure denominator, a time-to-event record, or a link between sequenced samples and the normal-development/survival curve. Sample counts are not survival counts.

NCBI classifies the series as high-throughput expression profiling and links sample records to SRA experiments. The inspected E8.5 ex-utero and mESC SRA records both carry generic RNA-Seq/transcriptomic/cDNA/paired-end labels. Those fields do not identify bulk versus single-cell or assign the experiments to the paper's separate RNA-seq analyses. The paper states that both bulk and single-cell data are in GSE149372, but the sample-to-assay and sample-to-figure mapping remains unresolved. The metadata snapshots are pinned by hash in the structured inventory; a later request returned NCBI's API rate limit, so no retry was made.

For the feasibility map, this is an important stage transition: a system can reproduce substantial early development and still fail when the growing embryo's extraembryonic exchange support is insufficient. The observation motivates a specific evidence need—unit-linked serial morphology, circulation, adverse-event, and disposition data at the late-organogenesis boundary—without establishing a solution or human operating target. It does not demonstrate live birth, complete gestation, or postnatal health. The separate E12.5 mouse preprint and ovine fetal-support cohorts remain distinct evidence windows, with no linked embryo or animal trajectory.
