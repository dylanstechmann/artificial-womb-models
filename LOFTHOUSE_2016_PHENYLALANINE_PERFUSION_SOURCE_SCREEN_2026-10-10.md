# Lofthouse et al. 2016 phenylalanine perfusion source screen — 2026-10-10

## Result

This is a biologically relevant aggregate comparator for placental amino-acid uptake, fetal delivery, and tissue retention, but not a source-linked observation dataset. The accessible nine-page full-text PDF reports group summaries for five term placentas (figures use mean ± SE) and no individual-perfusion measurements, machine-readable supplementary table, or data repository surfaced in this bounded screen. Keep it as aggregate evidence only; do not digitize curves into unit-level values.

## Evidence and source-quality findings

The study uses perfused full-term human placental tissue and labelled phenylalanine to examine maternal placental uptake, fetal-side delivery, flow relationships, and tissue/protein retention. It reports n=5 and means ± SE for the experimental figures. The authors report that placental uptake varied with maternal flow while fetal delivery did not show a corresponding flow relationship, and discuss tissue metabolism/protein incorporation as a possible determinant. Their model extrapolation to physiological amino-acid concentrations is explicitly not an experimental result.

The paper contains a material source-unit discrepancy: Methods (printed page R829) gives the tracer concentration as 2.7 nmol/L; Discussion (printed page R834) refers to 2.7 pmol/L. Preserve both statements unresolved; do not use either as a calibrated concentration input.

The article reports an aggregate tissue accounting result in which the recovered free and protein-associated label amounted to 56% of the tracer it says was retained in tissue. This is a group summary, not a closed per-perfusion balance; individual uptake, fetal transfer, tissue retention, incorporation, and recovery values were not reported in the inspected PDF.

## Feasibility relevance and limits

The source supports a useful interface requirement: a transfer assessment must distinguish maternal uptake, fetal delivery, placental retention, and metabolic incorporation. Concentration or maternal disappearance alone cannot establish delivery to a developing unit. Any future nutrient-transport candidate needs unit-linked source measurements, explicit units and isotope basis, tissue fate, complete disposition/recovery accounting, and independently reviewable raw observations. This term-placenta study does not establish early embryonic exchange, developmental support, continuous gestation, birth, or health. No author was contacted.
