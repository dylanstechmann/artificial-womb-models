# Public placental-perfusion data repository screen

**Screen date:** 2026-10-10  
**Purpose:** Find public, per-run human placental-transfer observations that pair concentration time courses with compartment-volume or sample-removal records.  
**Scope:** Bounded discovery searches of Figshare, Dryad, Zenodo, Mendeley Data and OSF, followed by inspection of promising repository records. No authors were contacted. Search results are leads, not evidence that no other dataset exists.

## Eligibility question

Could an openly accessible repository record support a source-exact observation table with the experimental unit, time, compartment, analyte, concentration/unit and the volume/sampling history needed to interpret changing amounts? A repository entry that exposes only a paper or group summary is retained as a lead, not treated as raw observations. Pregnancy PBPK, tissue imaging, transcriptomics and placenta-on-chip molecular profiles remain separate evidence lanes.

## Result and next gate

This bounded repository search found no public archive that exposes the required linked, per-run human placental concentration and volume/removal histories. It did find useful partial records: published concentrations, group-level compartment-volume curves, end-of-run volume-loss summaries, assay-QC summaries, placental metabolic outcomes, structural imaging and a separate pregnancy PBPK dataset. These cannot be pooled or substituted for one another.

The immediate intake gate remains the same: require explicit unit identity, source locators, original units and times, sample/removal history, compartment volumes, missingness and assay uncertainty before a dimensional transport fit. The PBPK repository may be screened as a separate model-validation source if the project later turns to pregnancy concentration predictions; its repository metadata alone do not qualify the individual files or their endpoints.

No author contact was made. A failed repository search is not proof that data do not exist elsewhere or were never measured.
