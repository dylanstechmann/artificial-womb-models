The next feasibility target is an interface whose measured functions support

This document freezes two narrow questions and their advancement conditions.

## IF-1: Does a core trace distinguish exchange from internal transfer?

The existing two-compartment fixture has equal abstract capacities, a known
boundary input, exchange `e`, internal transfer `d` and common loss `l`:

```text
x' = e*(b-x) - d*(x-y) - l*x
y' = d*(x-y) - l*y
```

Eliminating the interface state `x` gives:

```text
y'' + (e+2*d+2*l)*y' + (e*d+l*(e+2*d)+l*l)*y = e*d*b(t)
```

For zero initial states, the transformation `(e,d,l) -> (2*d,e/2,l)`
preserves the equation, input and both initial conditions for `y`.
Consequently, a known boundary history plus a complete, noiseless core trace
still cannot globally distinguish those rate allocations. At `e=2*d` the
swap coincides; it supplies no distinct witness at that particular point.

The new [invented fixture](examples/transport_identifiability_fixture.json)
uses unequal allocations. `transport-identifiability` exports both exact
core traces and both interface traces, the scalar coefficients, a second
equilibrium ambiguity and valid witness configurations. It uses the
existing independently checked closed-form reference. The algebraic identity
establishes equivalence; a numerical tolerance only checks its evaluation.

There is a second ambiguity: multiplying all rates by a positive common
factor preserves both equilibrium states. Spatial observations at equilibrium
cannot supply the missing rate scale. With zero initial states and no input,
both traces remain zero for every rate allocation.

| Observation contract | What this diagnostic establishes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known boundary and core trace, zero initial states | Exact swapped-rate ambiguity | Unique exchange/internal-transfer estimates |
| Both states at equilibrium | Common rate-scale ambiguity | Absolute rate recovery |
| Paired transient interface/core states with known boundary | The unequal swapped pair has different interface traces | Identification of all rates with noisy data, unequal capacities or a wrong model |
| Paired states without excitation | Zero traces carry no rate information | Rate recovery from more repeated zero readings |

**Frozen software question:** can the same supplied observation trace be
generated by a different rate allocation within this exact model class?
The supplied fixture answers yes for core-only data. Paired transients
distinguish this pair, subject to the stated assumptions.

**Next measured-data question:** which effective exchange/loss quantities
can be recovered from source-linked, independently observed boundary,
interface and internal quantities over time, compared against a simpler
reference? Calibrated units, independent-unit identities, measured collection,
geometry/capacities, uncertainty and a frozen unit split remain necessary.
One published Papp endpoint cannot populate that contract. Even a raw core
time series would need its rate-allocation ambiguity assessed before fitting.

**Advancement condition:** an eligible existing dataset and human-reviewed
model/observation mapping, followed by a held-out comparison that exposes
indistinguishable allocations and unavailable estimates. This diagnostic
sets no observation cadence, physiological threshold or device parameter.
Its shared loss does not identify uptake as a biochemical mechanism or
separate uptake from adsorption or other omitted losses.

## IF-2: Does interface identity imply developmental function?

[Molè et al., *Modeling human embryo implantation in vitro*, Cell
189, 87–105.e28 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.10.027)
provides a closer starting point for the early-interface gate than term
placental permeability alone. Its [primary paper copy](https://zenodo.org/records/20022561)
was inspected, with Figures 7 and S7 checked in text and rendered pages.
The paper reports early development through day 14 after fertilization,
predicted cell interactions, and reduced trophoblast outgrowth following
an AXL-directed signaling perturbation. Embryos and blastoids are separate
populations, and predictions from expression are separate from perturbation
outcomes.

The main Figure 7G caption reports 15 implanted embryos in the control
group and 13 in the perturbed group across two independent experiments.
Its outcomes are HLA-G-positive area above a threshold and outgrowth length;
the displayed units are mm² and µm. The separate blastoid result in Figure
S7G has four per group. Figure S7H uses a different embryo subset for
epiblast area and reports an outlier exclusion. These denominators cannot
be pooled or treated as the complete starting embryo cohort. No plotted
points were digitized into supposed raw observations here.

**Design implication, as an inference:** investigate maternal-facing tissue
interactions alongside bulk exchange, and retain a hybrid living-interface
hypothesis. The result does not establish a required machine intervention,
an exclusively AXL-mediated mechanism or later-stage adequacy. The authors
identify incomplete immune/vascular representation and unresolved TAM-family
receptor roles among their limitations. Morphology and lineage expression
do not measure sustained supply, removal, vascular function or later growth.

**Frozen biological question:** which published evidence supports a
functional embryo–endometrial interaction beyond cell identity alone?
Compare the author-reported perturbation/outgrowth result with expression-
based interaction predictions and the separately reported growth controls.
Alternative explanations include context-dependent signaling, broader
receptor effects and image/selection or cohort differences. The reported
controls narrow some alternatives but do not independently establish an
exclusive mechanism.

**Reanalysis gate:** require original Figure 7G values and their specimen,
experiment, endometrial-source and endpoint linkage; original image-analysis
units/thresholds and uncertainty; assignment/exclusions and starting-cohort
denominators; and human source review. Preserve the separate Figure S7
populations and exclusions. Do not attach transcriptomic cell rows to a
phenotype unless the source establishes that linkage. Until then, the result
is a candidate source/functional-evidence card, not a unit-level causal
reanalysis or continuous-gestation result. No request was sent to authors.

## One additional transport candidate was checked

[Vachalova et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopha.2024.117399)
has a [public three-workbook dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/15387278)
with a CC BY 4.0 repository declaration. The downloaded files contain human
term-placenta fragment/cell endpoints and rat term-placenta extraction-ratio
endpoints. Species and model classes remain separate. This term-stage
material cannot establish early human implantation continuity.

The perfusion workbook repeats extraction-ratio values in an overall block
and male/female blocks: the combined male/female value multiset matches the
overall block in each of its six columns. Those are overlapping presentations, not independent
additional observations. Its rows do not carry observation times or source
unit identifiers. The human endpoints include normalized controls rather
than fully supplied paired raw controls. The inspected workbooks therefore
do not resolve IF-1's paired dynamic observation contract. No inferred
pairing, sample independence, kinetic parameters or treatment advice is
derived from them.
