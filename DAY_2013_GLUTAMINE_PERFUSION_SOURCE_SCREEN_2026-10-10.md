# Day et al. 2013 glutamine perfusion source screen — 2026-10-10

## Result

This is a high-value mechanism and aggregate-flux comparator for placental amino-acid metabolism, but it is not a row-level observation dataset. The public article and supplement report group summaries from short term-human-cotyledon perfusions; no individual run trajectories or complete per-run mass balance were located. The source is licensed CC BY-NC-ND 3.0, so this screen records metadata and conclusions without reproducing its tables or figures as a derived dataset. No author was contacted.

## Sources inspected

- Day et al., [*Partitioning of glutamine synthesised by the isolated perfused human placenta between the maternal and fetal circulations*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3851744/), *Placenta* 34(12):1223–1231 (2013), DOI [10.1016/j.placenta.2013.10.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.placenta.2013.10.003). The public article is marked CC BY-NC-ND 3.0.
- The article's public [supplement, mmc1.docx](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S0143400413007753-mmc1.docx), contains four supplementary figures. It was inspected in memory and not retained locally.
- An exact-DOI/data search surfaced an OmicsDI record that describes the publication as literature metadata, not an independent experimental dataset. No separate run-level data archive surfaced in this bounded search.

## Evidence and source-quality findings

The paper reports dual ex-vivo perfusion of term human placental cotyledons under four labeled-substrate protocols: fetal U-13C-glutamate, maternal 15N-leucine, fetal 15N-glutamate, and maternal 15N-glutamate. The published methods describe fixed maternal and fetal flow rates, hourly venous sampling over five hours, and tissue measurement at the end of perfusion. These are study-specific methods, not operating recommendations.

The article's tables report group-level uptake and tissue amino-acid summaries with group sizes of four to six, depending on protocol and endpoint. The supplement contains mean±SEM figures; S1 and S2 report n=4, S3 shows a time course, and S4 compares lipid isotope spectra in n=4 tissue samples. None provides source-located individual timepoint values. The article does not provide a complete per-run join among input tracer, maternal/fetal outflow, tissue pools, and recovered label.

The authors report that glutamate from both circulations was largely converted into glutamine and that most synthesized glutamine appeared in the maternal circulation. The article estimates the maternal glutamine release at about 7 mmol/day and compares it with maternal flux and placental delivery; that is an author-reported extrapolation, not an independently reconstructed unit-level measurement. The result illustrates why substrate uptake, metabolic conversion, tissue retention, and delivery to the fetal circuit must be measured as separate destinations.

## Feasibility relevance and limits

This source supports a concrete interface-accounting requirement: quantify metabolism and partitioning across both exchange circuits and tissue, because maternal uptake alone cannot establish fetal delivery. It is a short-duration ex-vivo study of term tissue and does not demonstrate nutrient adequacy, embryo development, continuous gestation, birth, or later health. Keep it as an aggregate mechanism comparator; do not use the reported group summaries to calibrate an early-development support model or infer a long-duration operating regime.
