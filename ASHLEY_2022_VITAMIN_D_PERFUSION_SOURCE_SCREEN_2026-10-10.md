# Ashley et al. 2022 vitamin-D placental-perfusion source screen

**Disposition:** term human placental metabolism/transfer candidate; source-data workbook pinned and 30 per-perfusion endpoint records structured; no author contacted.

The [primary article](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.71094) reports five fresh term human placentas studied with an open-loop ex-vivo cotyledon perfusion for five hours using isotope-labeled 25(OH)D3. Its [Figure 1 source-data workbook](https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/71094/elife-71094-fig1-data1-v1.xlsx) contains one worksheet (`Sheet1`, A1:T34) and no formulas. The exact 11,903-byte file is preserved at `artifacts/ashley-2022-vitamin-d-perfusion/figure1-source-data.xlsx`; `SOURCE.json` records the official URL and SHA-256 `68eeb11636c5247751541cd48f5520c871666e4142401a77d7bc65580e69477a`.

The candidate intake selects 20 per-perfusion 13C-25(OH)D3 endpoints (maternal loss, metabolism, placental retention, and fetal-side appearance; five labels per endpoint) and 10 per-perfusion 1,25(OH)2D3 maternal/fetal rates, using the workbook's explicit `fmol/min/g` columns. Each record points to its exact source cell and retains the perfusion label as a study identifier, not a donor identity. The article describes five placentas and reports the same 25D group means±SEM as the workbook to rounding: maternal loss 3.01±1.12, metabolism 2.26±0.97, placental retention 0.45±0.27, and fetal appearance 0.30±0.10 pmol/min/g. The source values for the four 25D components sum to the recorded maternal-loss value within each run. The 1,25D workbook means±SEM are 0.201±0.048 fmol/min/g maternal and 0.0400±0.0084 fetal, matching the article to rounding. These are source-mapping checks only; the 25D components may share calculations and are not independent validations or model targets.

One important conflict remains outside the imported observations. The worksheet labels the 24,25(OH)2D3 block `nmol/min/g`, while the article reports `pmol/min/g`. Its maternal workbook mean is 2.4986±0.3537 (SEM), whereas the article reports 2.05±0.35; the fetal means agree after rounding (0.7064 versus 0.71), but the workbook SEM (0.2196) differs from the article's 0.10. The placental mean/SEM agree after rounding (0.5031±0.3017 versus 0.50±0.30). All 24,25 values and group summaries remain out of the observation dataset pending review; the cell audit preserves their locations and literals. A separate row labeled only `placenta` for 1,25(OH)2D3 is also excluded because its per-perfusion linkage is unclear, despite the article's note that this production measure had n=1 due to detection limits.

The source-cell ledger at `config/source_inventories/ashley_2022_figure1_source_cells.csv` records all 207 nonempty workbook cells, their coordinates, raw cell values and current intake disposition. The structured dataset at `config/observations/ashley_2022_vitamin_d_perfusion_candidate.json` validates as a candidate intake, and the validator's local SHA-256 check matches the pinned workbook. The article also states that perfusion identifies delivery but not accumulation in either open-loop circulation. These are term ex-vivo metabolic endpoints, not raw concentration-time series, embryo culture, developmental outcomes, evidence of nutrient sufficiency, or artificial-gestation performance. Do not pool them with other studies or fit a dimensional support model. Source transcription and scientific review remain pending.
