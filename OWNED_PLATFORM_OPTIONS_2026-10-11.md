## Decision to make

The objective remains owner-controlled equipment capable eventually of ten simultaneous human gestations from the declared embryo starting boundary, without a human surrogate, with same-calendar-date births. The route decision is how to build an owner-controlled platform while the scientific starting-boundary and sustained-support gaps are still open. No route in this memo establishes that the required machine is currently feasible, available to buy, or achievable on a two-to-three-year schedule. A 100% success guarantee is not supported.

**Current planning recommendation:** preserve owner-led control of the requirements, data and final configuration, while keeping acquisition/licensing and co-development open as ways to obtain components and specialist capability. Do not choose a supplier or purchase major equipment until the starting boundary, subsystem deliverable, access/ownership rights, qualified operator, independent evidence and written quote are concrete. This keeps the owner's priority on ownership while avoiding the assumption that a prematurity-support device covers the embryo-to-birth gap.

## Ownership conditions for any route

Before owner money or schedule is committed to a co-developed or acquired subsystem, the written package must state:

1. What exact subsystem and developmental boundary the deliverable covers, what measured results support that boundary, and what outcomes remain unknown.
2. Who holds title to each physical asset, software/configuration, design file, source record and resulting data; what third-party restrictions follow the asset.
3. Who can operate, inspect, modify, repair, calibrate, maintain and replace the equipment; access to source files, interfaces, parts, technical records and training must survive the original supplier relationship.
4. Verification deliverables, independent review, acceptance criteria, exclusions, milestone dates and what happens after failure, delay, supplier exit or termination.
5. Full cost basis: setup, facility fit, personnel, ongoing operation, maintenance, consumables, replacement, qualification and data/IP terms. Each amount requires an attributable quote or an explicitly labeled estimate.

If any essential control right is missing, classify that route as access to a service or a dependency, not as owner-controlled equipment. This contract checklist is a planning requirement, not legal advice.
